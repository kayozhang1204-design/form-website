The problem

Most campaigns run across too many tools.

A typical launch moves through planning decks, shared drives, email approvals and a separate report for each channel. That causes three problems.

  1. 1

    You pay to reach the same people twice.

    Each channel buys its own audience. The same customers see the ad on several platforms, while people who have never heard of you are missed.

  2. 2

    Launches slip while approvals repeat.

    Every format is reviewed on its own. One change to the message restarts the review for all the others.

  3. 3

    No one can say what worked.

    Each platform reports results in its own way. It is hard to tell which spend actually brought in new customers.

FORM replaces these hand-offs with one workflow your whole team shares.

How it works

One workflow from brief to results.

Every campaign follows the same four steps, and each step uses what the team agreed in the one before.

  1. Plan

    Agree on the audience, the business goal and the budget in one shared brief before choosing channels.

  2. Create

    Build every ad format from that brief. Reviewers approve the core message once, then check only what changed.

  3. Launch

    Run the campaign across TV, mobile, tablet and desktop with one view of reach, so overlap shows up early.

  4. Measure

    Compare results with the goal in the brief, and use holdout tests to see which sales the ads added.

One message, adapted for every screen your customers use.

In the Launch step, FORM shows how the same campaign reaches people across devices, so you can see who has already seen it.

Because every step starts from the same brief, results can be checked against the goals you set.

Results

What your team can measure.

Set targets for these three outcomes in the brief, then track them while the campaign runs and after it ends.

  • Cost of each new customer

    See what each channel spends to win a new customer, and move budget away from people you already reach.

    • Cost per new customer
    • New versus returning customers
    • Audience overlap between channels

  • Time to launch

    Track how long a campaign takes from approved brief to live ads, and which reviews hold it up.

    • Days from brief to launch
    • Review rounds per format

  • Sales the ads added

    Holdout tests compare people who saw the ads with similar people who did not. The difference estimates the sales the campaign added.

    • Added sales
    • Return on ad spend

Walk through an example

Sample scenario

A spring launch for an outdoor clothing brand.

Field Notes is a made-up brand we use to show the workflow. Its team wants to win new customers aged 25 to 40 with a spring campaign on streaming TV, social and mobile.

  1. Plan

    The brief sets one goal, cost per new customer, and splits the budget across three channels.

  2. Create

    The team writes two campaign ideas and adapts them into six ad formats. Reviewers approve each idea once.

  3. Launch

    In week one, FORM shows that social and mobile audiences overlap heavily. The team moves part of that budget to streaming TV.

  4. Measure

    A holdout test in two regions estimates how many sales the campaign added. Those results shape the summer brief.

This scenario shows how the workflow fits together. It does not report results from a real customer.

Getting this right matters more as budgets move into newer channels.
